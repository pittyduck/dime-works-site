From Grid Findings to the Contract: Data Center Lease and Deal Terms in Korea

Last in our data center series. Seven articles have produced a particular kind of finding — a grid filing at a stage, a renewable claim of a certain type, a permit calendar with gaps, an efficiency figure with a boundary. None of it survives signing unless it becomes a term. This closing article applies the conversion discipline from our ESG series to the asset class where the findings are least like the ones a lease was drafted for: the ones about power.

Published 19 November 2026 · Dime Works · Reading time ~7 min

Key takeaways

  • Every surviving data center finding resolves into one of the same four contractual shapes — a delivery obligation, a verification right, a data-access term, or a remedy structure — but on this asset class the delivery obligation is usually about megawatts, and the remedy is usually price or exit rather than repair.
  • Grid status belongs in conditions and long-stop dates, not in warranties. A warranty that power is "secured" is a dispute; a condition keyed to a named assessment stage, an approved capacity, and a date is a mechanism.
  • Approved capacity below filed capacity is a price term. The model was built on the filing; the contract should be built on the approval, with an adjustment for the difference and for any corrective measures attached.
  • Renewable positions need holder and assignment terms: who holds the contract, whether it follows the asset on a change of control, and what the tenant's covenant actually requires the landlord to deliver.
  • The as-of problem is a contract problem. Where a finding depends on a newly codified rule or a statute whose subordinate rules are still being drafted, the agreement needs a re-verification right at signing and a regulatory-change mechanism across the March 2027 effective date.

The same four shapes, a different subject

Our earlier article on converting ESG findings into contract terms argued that a finding which fits none of four shapes — delivery obligation, verification right, data-access term, remedy structure — was never really a finding. That framework holds for a data center. What changes is the subject matter. On a conventional asset the delivery obligation is about a building attribute and the remedy is about a fix. On a data center the delivery obligation is about capacity, timing, and permits, and the remedy is about money or the right to walk away, because a grid segment cannot be repaired by the seller.

Grid status: conditions, not warranties

The most common drafting error on this asset class is to take the grid finding — "assessment filed," "first-stage review passed," "supply approved" — and place it in a warranty. A warranty that power has been secured is a promise about a fact the seller does not control and the buyer cannot verify without the utility; if it turns out to be wrong, the buyer has a claim, not a data center. The finding belongs instead in the mechanism of the deal: a condition precedent keyed to a named stage of the assessment process — full review entered, supply approval issued — with the approved capacity and the approval date as defined terms; a long-stop date after which the buyer may withdraw; and, on a forward purchase, a phasing of the price against the stages actually reached.

The corrective-measures mechanism deserves its own clause. Approval with reduced or phased capacity, a substation land contribution, or a different connection voltage is a different project from the one priced. The agreement should say what happens if the approved facility differs from the filed one: a price adjustment formula per megawatt, a threshold below which the buyer may walk, and an allocation of the cost of any corrective works.

Exemption and location: regulatory-change terms

A non-capital-region project priced on the relief the AI data center legislation will provide from March 2027 carries a dependency the contract should name: that the Act enters into force as scheduled, that its subordinate rules bring the project within the exemption and definitions, and that any special-zone designation the pricing assumed is actually made. Each is a condition or a price term. And because the assessment's own rule changed as recently as November 2026 — when the implementing notice took effect and raised the capital-region pass mark — a finding obtained under one version may not survive the next: which is a re-verification right at signing, and a regulatory-change clause allocating the consequence if the rule moves between signing and completion.

Renewable supply: holder, assignment, covenant

The renewable verification article ended with four things to establish — instrument, document, delivery, holder. In the contract, the holder is the term that matters most. A landlord's power purchase agreement that does not follow the asset on a change of control is a finding that evaporates at completion unless the agreement requires the counterparty's consent to assignment to be obtained as a condition, or prices its absence. A tenant's renewable covenant should be read for what it actually obliges the landlord to deliver — a signed instrument of a stated type, by a date, for a stated share of load — and the data-access term should require the metered supply and the accounting basis to be provided on a schedule, so that the claim can be tested through the term rather than asserted at signing. Where the supply plan relies on the Act's direct-supply channel, the same regulatory-change mechanism applies.

Permits and plant: completion conditions

The backup power and construction-phase articles described a facility that can be physically complete and not yet lawful to operate. On a forward purchase, that becomes the completion condition: every operating permit — emissions filing, specified soil-facility registration, dangerous-substance storage permit, discharge arrangement — in force before the equipment it governs is run, and before the price for that phase is paid. On an operating asset it becomes a reconciliation warranty backed by a data-access right: the three registers reconciled to installed equipment, with the inspection histories delivered, and a remedy where they do not reconcile.

Efficiency and water: data-access terms

A quoted PUE and a water balance are not warranties either; they are figures with boundaries. The contractual home for them is the data-access term — metered power and water at a stated boundary and period, delivered on a schedule through the term — together with, where a tenant or lender applies an external benchmark, a reporting covenant that produces the figures in the benchmark's own format. That converts a point-in-time claim into a record the parties can each read, which is the only form in which an efficiency figure has contractual meaning.

Closing the series

Eight articles on one asset class is a long way to travel to reach a conversion table. The reason is that the table only works if the findings feeding it are the right findings — and on a Korean data center, the right findings are not the ones a general environmental review produces. They are about a grid filing's stage, a statute's effective date, a contract's holder, a permit's sequencing, and a metric's boundary. We have set out where each comes from. What remains is the discipline this article describes: that a finding which cannot be written as a condition, a right, a data term, or a remedy has not yet been finished — and that on this asset class, the unfinished ones are usually the ones about power.

Scope limitations and uncertainty

This article is general information, not legal advice, and does not draft or recommend specific contract language; the contractual shapes described are analytical categories for organizing findings, and their expression in any agreement is a matter for the parties' Korean counsel. Regulatory references — the grid impact assessment's stages, the AI data center legislation's effective date and provisions, and permit categories — are summarized as set out in earlier articles in this series and carry the same limitations, including that the assessment's implementing notice and the Act's subordinate legislation were not finalized at the time of writing. Korean-language texts control. Site-specific and transaction-specific conclusions require an engagement, not an article.
